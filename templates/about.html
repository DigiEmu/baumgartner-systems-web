01 Mission

Technology should be verifiable, not merely trusted.

Organisations increasingly rely on systems whose outputs cannot be examined after the fact. Decisions are made on the basis of records that may be incomplete, transformed or impossible to replay.

We build infrastructure that makes evidence, provenance and system state explicit — so that what happened inside an AI-assisted workflow can be reconstructed, checked and explained, within a boundary that is itself documented.

02 Expertise

What we work on.

Independent Verification

Examining whether recorded evidence, provenance and results can be independently verified within an explicitly defined boundary.

Evidence Infrastructure

Systems that capture and preserve provenance, integrity and state so that workflows produce reviewable, reconstructable records.

Interoperability

Integration boundaries, evidence portability and verification workflows designed to fit existing systems rather than replace them.

Research & Development

Ongoing development of the DigiEmu technology stack — from evidence workflows to verifiable state compression.

03 Principles

How we work.

Independence

A verification result should stand on its own — not on the reputation of whoever produced it.

Reproducibility

A result that cannot be replayed and checked is a claim, not evidence.

Transparency

Boundaries, methods and limitations are documented, not implied.

Human Responsibility

Infrastructure supports accountable human decisions; it does not replace them.

Responsible Development

Capabilities are developed and presented with their limits stated — including what they cannot prove.

04 Founder

Bruno Baumgartner

Swiss software developer and independent technology researcher.

He develops the DigiEmu technology stack and the verification infrastructure behind Baumgartner Digital Infrastructure. His work focuses on evidence, provenance and reconstructability in AI-assisted systems — on the engineering premise that claims about software should be testable, and that the limits of a result matter as much as the result itself.
